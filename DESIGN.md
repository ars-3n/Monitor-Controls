# Monitor Controls

Monitor Controls is used at arm’s length from an external display, often during a call. Opening the menu should immediately answer which display, output, and microphone a control will change.

## Readability

Use the standard macOS body style for controls, values, status messages, and explanatory text. At the default Mac setting this is 13 pt. Use the system headline weight for device names and a modest title for the panel and Settings pages. Use tabular digits for percentages. Give a long name another line or let a region scroll before reducing type. Keep control targets comfortable independently of their label size.

## Menu bar and panel

Use the square AppKit status item with the Sound Levels template icon. Keep Settings visible in the top row. The display’s preview varies with hardware brightness. The numeric value and labeled slider communicate the same setting. Each audio channel identifies its current device, offers the device menu, and exposes its mute and level controls when supported.

## Materials and interaction

Use native Liquid Glass for interactive controls and Settings navigation on macOS 26 and later. Earlier releases use standard macOS materials and buttons. System text colors, accent color, focus rings, and menus follow the user’s appearance preferences. Show mute, connection, and visibility states in words or symbols as well as color. [Apple’s guidance on adopting Liquid Glass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/adopting-liquid-glass)

Keep the Settings window stable when switching sections. Device lists scroll inside it. Reordering and visibility are available by button and keyboard. Open the monitor pairing control directly when someone follows the panel’s pairing link.
